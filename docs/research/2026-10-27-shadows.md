# Shadows and elevation: what the sources say

Research for a principle page on box-shadow. The question came from Post (the playbook's mail app): "is the shadow too heavy?" Post uses a desk-and-raised-card look. It has a warm tinted ground, the mail on one raised surface, and the tools floating as a pill above it.

**Verified** means I read the claim in the source at the URL given. **Secondary** means I read it only in a summary, a third-party page, or a search-engine digest. **Unverified** means it comes from memory or common lore and I did not find a source. Sources were collected on 2026-10-26.

---

## 0. Post today (for calibration)

Observed in the source, 2026-10-26:

- **The card** (`src/demos/post/Shell.module.scss:140`): `0 1px 2px α.04, 0 4px 16px α.05`, plus a 1px hairline ring at α.04. The shadow colour is warm (`oklch(0.35 0.02 75)`). In dark mode the ring becomes `var(--line)`.
- **The floating pill** (`src/demos/post/Conversation.module.scss:27`): a 1px ring at α.07, then `0 2px 6px α.05`, with `backdrop-filter: blur(12px)`.

Compared with the systems below, Post's card shadow is already *lighter* than every reference:

| Shadow | Total alpha | Key offset : blur |
|---|---|---|
| Fluent `shadow4` | .12 + .14 | 2 : 4 |
| Material 3 level 1 | key .3 × 1, ambient .15 × 1 (multiplied into the shadow colour) | — |
| Post card | ≈ .09 | 4 : 16, so the offset is blur/4, not blur/2 |

So "too heavy" is probably not about alpha. The likelier problems are geometry and the relation between the two layers (see Mechanisms 1 and 4).

## 1. Perception

### 1.1 Light-from-above, and how weak it is

- **Ramachandran (1988), *Perception of shape from shading*, Nature 331:163.** https://doi.org/10.1038/331163a0 (abstract read via Europe PMC). **Verified (abstract).** Shape-from-shading "assumes that there is only one light source illuminating the entire visual image". Two identically shaded objects lit from different angles cannot both be seen in 3D at once.
- **Kleffner & Ramachandran (1992), Perception & Psychophysics.** https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03206757. **Verified (abstract).** The two constraints are a single light source and light from "above" *in retinal coordinates*. Shading-defined convex tokens pop out in search.
- **Sun & Perona (1998), *Where is the sun?*, Nature Neuroscience 1:183.** https://doi.org/10.1038/630. The 1996 symposium version is at https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/32233-1te38. **Verified (Caltech abstract summary).** Search performance is best for lighting from the top-left, about 20° from vertical. Both handedness groups show it, and right-handers lean further left.
- **Mamassian & Goutcher (2001), *Prior knowledge on the illumination position*, Cognition.** https://doi.org/10.1016/s0010-0277(01)00116-0. **Verified (abstract).** The prior is biased above-left, reaching "26 degrees on average" in a shape-discrimination task.
- **Adams, Graf & Ernst (2004), *Experience can change the 'light-from-above' prior*, Nature Neuroscience 7:1057.** https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1312. **Verified (abstract).** Active (haptic) experience shifts the prior, and the shift generalises to a different task: "priors are constantly adapted by interactive experience". I did not read the magnitude of the shift.
- **Morgenstern, Murray & Harris (2011), *The human visual system's assumption that light comes from above is weak*, PNAS.** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1100794108 (PMC3145687). **Verified (abstract).** The prior "receives surprisingly little weight and can be overridden by lighting cues that are barely perceptible."

What this means for UI: the light-from-above prior governs *shading* (bevels, insets, gradients, convex versus concave). For a flat card it is a weak default that any visible cue overrides. The rule that does survive is Ramachandran's single light source.

### 1.2 Cast shadows convey position, especially in motion

- **Kersten, Mamassian & Knill (1997), *Moving cast shadows induce apparent motion in depth*, Perception 26:171.** https://doi.org/10.1068/p260171. **Verified (abstract).**
  - Shadow motion "overrides other strong sources of information", such as constant size.
  - "The natural constraint of shadow darkness plays a role", although under some conditions even light (unnatural) shadows work.
  - A moving light source is misread as a moving object, which supports a stationary-light-source constraint.
- **Kersten lab demos.** https://kerstenlab.psych.umn.edu/demos/shadows. **Verified.** When the shadow's path is horizontal, the ball "appears to rise above the floor". Reversing contrast, so the shadow is a light patch, weakens the effect, and so does lighting from below.
- **Mamassian, Knill & Kersten (1998), *The perception of cast shadows*, TICS 2:288.** https://doi.org/10.1016/s1364-6613(98)01204-2. **Verified (abstract).** Cast shadows matter most for "spatial arrangement", meaning where the object sits relative to the background, especially when the shadow moves.
- **Allen (1999), Perceptual and Motor Skills 89:571.** https://doi.org/10.2466/pms.1999.89.2.571. **Verified (abstract).** With real shadows, a thin raised rod is correctly judged closer. Shadows "had to overwhelm competing height and stimulus-size cues."
- **Ni, Braunstein & Andersen (2004), Perception 33.** https://doi.org/10.1068/p5288. **Verified (abstract).** A dark "second object" behaves more like a shadow. *Darkness* is part of what makes a patch read as a shadow.
- **Ouhnana & Kingdom (2016), i-Perception.** https://doi.org/10.1177/2041669516677843. **Verified (abstract).** The cast-shadow effect "holds even if the cast shadow appears unrealistic". In the ball-in-box task, shadows still outweighed a second object.

### 1.3 The numbers: offset carries height, blur barely does

**Cavanagh, Casati & Elder (2021), *Scaling depth from shadow offset*, Journal of Vision 21(12):15.** https://doi.org/10.1167/jov.21.12.15. Full text (CC BY) read via Europe PMC: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8631054/fullTextXML. **Verified (full text).** This is the key recent paper.

- **Method.** 25 observers set a marker to the perceived gap between a textured square and a background, using method of adjustment. There were 6 diagonal offsets (0.14–2.16 dva), 3 blur levels (0.02, 0.25 and 0.52 dva), and 4 directions. Shadows were a 50% luminance reduction.
- **Offset dominates.** Offset explained most of the variance (η² = .57).
  - Perceived depth ≈ **1.31 × offset**, or 1.40 when fitted through zero. That is equivalent to an assumed light about 35–38° off the surface normal.
  - Individual slopes ran from 0.88 to 2.01, but each observer was linear.
- **Blur barely matters for magnitude.** Unblurred shadows had a slope only **7.9% lower** than blurred ones, with η² = .0008.
  - The authors suggest blur may instead affect "whether depth is perceived at all, rather than the magnitude".
  - This fits Kersten's report that blurry shadows trigger motion-in-depth more often.
- **Direction barely matters.**
  - Right-side shadows (light from the left) added **1.6%** depth.
  - Shadows below (light from above) gave a slightly steeper slope.
  - The authors tie this to Morgenstern's "weak prior".
- **Lighter is not a shadow.** If the L-shaped patch is *lighter* than its surround, "there is no impression of a shadow and little or none of a separation" (Fig. 1).
- **Tight shadows compress depth.** In flash photos the shadow offset is tiny, and objects at arm's length look pinned to the wall (Fig. 2).

Implication for UI: perceived height ≈ 1.3–1.4 × y-offset. Blur and opacity mostly decide *whether* the patch reads as a soft real shadow, not *how high* the surface sits.

### 1.4 Ground contact

- **Adams et al. (2022), *Shedding Light on Cast Shadows*, IEEE TVCG.** https://doi.org/10.1109/tvcg.2021.3097978. **Verified (abstract).** Non-photorealistic shadow shading improved contact judgments in AR. There is a "tradeoff between photorealism and accuracy of depth perception". Stylised shadows can communicate better than physically correct ones.
- **Secondary** (search digest of the same paper): shadows raised confidence of contact only when the object really touched the ground.

### 1.5 Reviews

- Mamassian, Knill & Kersten (1998), above, is the standard review.
- Casati & Cavanagh, *The Visual World of Shadows* (MIT Press, 2019). **Unverified**: not read.
- I found no 2015+ replication of the light-from-above prior with UI stimuli. The 2021 JoV paper is the most recent quantitative cast-shadow study I found.

## 2. Interface evidence

- **NN/g, Kate Moran, *Flat UI Elements Attract Less Attention and Cause Uncertainty* (3 Sep 2017).** https://www.nngroup.com/articles/flat-ui-less-attention-cause-uncertainty/. **Verified.**
  - Method: 71 users with eyetracking on 9 sites, comparing weak-signifier and strong-signifier versions.
  - Weak-signifier pages took **22% longer**, with **25% more fixations** (p < .05).
  - Caveat: shadows were bundled with gradients and text treatments ("adding shadows, gradients, and text treatments to add depth"). The effect is *not* attributable to shadow alone.
  - Flat works with low density, a traditional layout and high-contrast targets.
- **Burmistrov et al. (2015), *Flat Design vs Traditional Design*, INTERACT, LNCS 9297:106.** https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-22668-2_10 (PDF: https://www.flatisbad.com/publications/Burmistrov-INTERACT15.pdf). **Secondary (abstract via search).** Flat icons took about 2× as long to find. Clickable objects on flat pages took longer and drew more errors. Again, shadows are confounded with every other "rich" cue.
- **Material Design low-vision research.** https://m2.material.io/design/environment/light-shadows.html (Research section). **Verified.**
  - 36 interviews with 15 low-vision participants.
  - "Using shadows and outlines increases the ease and speed of finding a component when scanning pages."
  - A shadow *or stroke outline* "improves one's ability to determine whether or not it can be interacted with."
  - Note the "or": outline and shadow are treated as interchangeable for this job.
- **Lucaites et al. (2017), Clemson course report.** http://andrewd.ces.clemson.edu/courses/cpsc412/fall17/teams/reports/group04.pdf. **Secondary.**
  - 20 participants; depth (shadow) and colour contrast had no significant effect on time-to-click.
  - Buttons with *neither* cue had worse search performance.
  - Not peer-reviewed.
- **Gap.** I found no study that varies shadow *strength* (alpha or blur) and measures perceived hierarchy, clickability or preference. Every value ramp in design systems is a design choice, not a measured optimum.

## 3. Design-system guidance

### 3.1 Material 3: colour first, shadow only when needed

From https://m3.material.io/styles/elevation/overview and https://m3.material.io/styles/elevation/applying-elevation, read in the browser. **Verified.**

- **Differences from M2.** "Instead of applying shadows by default to all levels, use shadows only when required to create additional protection against a background or to encourage interaction." The figure caption reads "M3: Using color instead of shadows to communicate elevation."
- **Levels.** There are six levels, 0 to +5.
  - "An element's resting state can be on levels 0 to +3, while levels +4 and +5 are reserved for user-interacted states such as hover and dragged."
  - Hovering raises a component one level; the FAB goes from level 3 to level 4.
  - "Tokens have no shadows or color; each platform determines the specific shadows."
- **What a surface must show.** "Surface edges… Overlap with other surfaces… Distance from other surfaces." "By default, Material 3's surfaces use tonal difference to indicate separation."
- **Shadow semantics.** "Both a shadow's size and amount of softness or diffusion express the degree of distance… small and sharp indicates… close proximity… Larger, softer shadows express more distance."
- **"When it comes to applying shadows, less is more. The fewer levels in your UI, the more power they have to direct attention and action."**
- **When to use visible shadows.** When "a background is patterned or visually busy, the hairline style might not provide sufficient protection," and to lift on interaction.
- **Scrims.** The scrim colour role is applied at 32%.
- **dp values.** Levels map to 0, 1, 3, 6, 8 and 12 dp.
  - Source: `material-web/tokens/versions/latest/sass/_md-sys-elevation.scss` (https://github.com/material-components/material-web/blob/main/tokens/versions/latest/sass/_md-sys-elevation.scss). **Verified.**
  - The overview text: "One surface at 1dp… another at 8dp… The difference in elevation… is 7dp". Elevation is *relative*.
- **Web implementation.** Source: https://github.com/material-components/material-web/blob/main/elevation/internal/_elevation.scss. **Verified.**
  - Two pseudo-elements: a **key** shadow at `opacity: 0.3` and an **ambient** shadow at `opacity: 0.15`.
  - Key, levels 1–5: `0 1 2 0`, `0 1 2 0`, `0 1 3 0`, `0 2 3 0`, `0 4 4 0`.
  - Ambient, levels 1–5: `0 1 3 1`, `0 2 6 2`, `0 4 8 3`, `0 6 10 4`, `0 8 12 6`.
  - The key layer stays small and tight. The ambient layer carries the growth, using positive spread.

### 3.2 Material 2: key and ambient light, and consistency

From https://m2.material.io/design/environment/light-shadows.html. **Verified.**

- "Key lights create sharper, directional shadows… Ambient light appears from all angles to create diffused, soft shadows." A key-only or ambient-only shadow is marked "Don't", and the combined shadow is "Do".
- "Because shadows express the degree of elevation between surfaces, they must be used consistently throughout your product."
- "When a surface changes shape or scale, but its elevation remains the same, its shadow should not change."
- "If your product doesn't use shadows, convey elevation in other ways, such as through parallax motion."

### 3.3 Fluent 2: a key + ambient ramp keyed to blur

From https://fluent2.microsoft.design/elevation, read in the browser. **Verified.**

- Elevation is used to "aid scannability, and convey levels of importance".
- "Consistent shadow direction conveys a perceived light source." "Sharp and crisp shadows indicate closeness… larger, softer shadows… greater distance."
- "Fluent shadows are created by combining sharp, directional shadows (key) to define the edges of an element and soft, diffused shadows (ambient) to imply distance."
- **"Windows uses strokes instead of key shadows to outline an object."**
- **Ramp.** Low ramp: 2, 4, 8, 16. High ramp: 28, 64.
  - Mapping: borderless cards at 4; FAB and raised cards at 8; callouts and hover cards at 16; bottom sheets and side navigation at 28; dialogs at 64.
- **Brand surfaces.** "The same shadow value on brand colors may not give the impression of the same elevation." The shadow opacity is recomputed from luminance: `Round(42 − 0.116·L)` and `Round(34 − 0.09·L)`.
- **Implementation.** **Verified** in the code.
  - Source: https://github.com/microsoft/fluentui/blob/master/packages/tokens/src/utils/shadows.ts.
  - Low ramp: `shadowN = 0 0 2px ambient, 0 (N/2)px Npx key`, for N = 2, 4, 8, 16.
  - High ramp: shadow28 = `0 0 8px ambient, 0 14px 28px key`; shadow64 = `0 0 8px ambient, 0 32px 64px key`.
  - **Key y-offset is exactly blur/2.**
  - Light colours (`packages/tokens/src/alias/lightColor.ts`): ambient `rgba(0,0,0,.12)`, key `.14`.
  - Dark colours (`darkColor.ts`): ambient `.24`, key `.28`. **Dark mode doubles the alpha.**
  - The website's formula table describes the two layers slightly differently from the code. Trust the code.

### 3.4 Apple: two planes and an adaptive shadow

- **HIG, Materials.** https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials (read via the HIG JSON). **Verified.**
  - "Liquid Glass forms a distinct functional layer for controls and navigation elements… that floats above the content layer."
  - "Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer."
  - Use standard materials (ultra-thin to thick) for distinction within content.
  - Over bright content, "consider adding a dark dimming layer of 35% opacity."
- **WWDC25 session 219, *Meet Liquid Glass*.** https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/ (transcript). **Verified.**
  - "Shadows also play an important role in helping elements feel grounded and defined."
  - "The element is aware of what's behind it and increases the opacity of its shadow when it is over text… lowers the opacity of its shadow when it is over a solid light background."
  - "As text scrolls underneath, shadows become more prominent to create additional separation."
  - The shadow is a *legibility* tool whose strength depends on what sits behind it, not a fixed elevation token.
- **HIG, Dark Mode.** https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode. **Verified.** "The system uses two sets of background colors — called base and elevated… base colors are dimmer, making background interfaces appear to recede, and the elevated colors are brighter, making foreground interfaces appear to advance." I did not verify the hex values.

### 3.5 Atlassian and IBM Carbon

- **Atlassian.** https://atlassian.design/foundations/elevation. **Verified.**
  - Levels are sunken, default, raised and overlay.
  - "Always pair `elevation.surface.raised` with `elevation.shadow.raised`." Surface and shadow are a single token pair.
  - "Shadows can be harder to see in dark mode, so dark mode elevations also rely on different surface colors… the higher the elevation, the lighter the surface looks."
  - "To create flat cards, pair with a border."
- **IBM Carbon.** https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/color/usage/. **Verified.** Four colour layers: base, then layers 01 to 03. Contextual layer tokens "call the correct values for that layer". The page says nothing about shadows. Carbon v9 had a 0–24 shadow scale (https://v9.carbondesignsystem.com/guidelines/layer, **secondary**).

### 3.6 Where the systems disagree, and why

1. **Is a shadow the default way to show elevation?**
   - M2 and Fluent say yes: every level has a shadow.
   - M3, Carbon and Atlassian's default level say no: colour or tone first, shadow by exception.
   - M3's stated reason: shadow power comes from scarcity ("the fewer levels… the more power").
2. **Does elevation mean importance?**
   - Fluent says it does ("convey levels of importance").
   - M3 says elevation is *distance*, which focuses attention only because the highest surface is on top. It warns against changing a component's default elevation.
3. **Dark mode.**
   - Fluent keeps shadows and doubles their alpha.
   - Material, Apple and Atlassian make higher surfaces lighter. Material and Atlassian keep a shadow as well.
   - M2 is explicit: "shadows remain dark… Don't use light glows in place of dark shadows".
4. **Edges.**
   - Windows replaces the key shadow with a stroke.
   - M3 calls the hairline the default protection and the shadow the fallback for busy backgrounds.
   - Material's low-vision research treats shadow and outline as interchangeable for findability.
5. **Fixed or adaptive.**
   - Every token system uses a fixed ramp.
   - Apple's Liquid Glass shadow changes opacity with the content underneath.
   - Fluent adapts to the *surface* colour (the luminance formula), not to what is behind it.

## 4. Dark mode

- **Why shadows fail.** A shadow can only darken. Against a near-black ground there is little luminance left to remove, so the contrast between shadow and ground collapses.
  - Material Android, `docs/theming/Dark.md` (https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Dark.md). **Verified.** "Shadows are less effective in an app using a dark theme, because they will have less contrast with the dark background colors… surfaces become lighter and more colorful at higher elevations, when they are closer to the implied light source."
  - The same file explains why the ground is grey rather than black: dark grey "increases visibility for shadows".
- **M2 overlay table.** https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme.html. **Verified.**
  - Recommended surface: `#121212`. "It's easier to see shadows on grey (instead of black)."
  - White overlay by elevation: 0dp 0%, 1dp 5%, 2dp 7%, 3dp 8%, 4dp 9%, 6dp 11%, 8dp 12%, 12dp 14%, 16dp 15%, 24dp 16%.
  - "An overlay… makes it easier to… see shadows. Overlays add contrast between surfaces and their shadows."
  - The table comes from `ElevationOverlayProvider.java`: `alpha% = 4.5 · ln(1 + dp) + 2`. **Verified** in the source (https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/lib/java/com/google/android/material/elevation/ElevationOverlayProvider.java). It is logarithmic, so the first dp counts most.
- **M3 replaced the overlays with fixed tonal surface roles.** Source: `docs/theming/Color.md` in the same repository. **Verified.**
  - Light tones: lowest 100, low 96, container 94, high 92, highest 90.
  - Dark tones: lowest 4, low 10, container 12, high 17, highest 22.
  - The roles "are not tied to elevation" (m3 applying-elevation page).
- **Apple:** base versus elevated backgrounds, as in §3.4. **Atlassian:** lighter surfaces plus the same shadows, as in §3.5. **Fluent:** doubled alpha, as in §3.3.
- **Glows are not shadows.** M2 says "Don't use light glows in place of dark shadows", and in Cavanagh 2021 Fig. 1 a light patch produces no depth. In both, a light halo reads as a mark, not as height.

## 5. Craft and rendering

### 5.1 Layering, ratio, colour

- **Josh Comeau, *Designing Beautiful Shadows in CSS*.** https://www.joshwcomeau.com/css/designing-shadows/. **Verified.**
  - Single light source: "Each element on the page should be lit from the same global light source"; "every shadow on the page should share the same ratio".
  - As an element rises, offset and blur grow while opacity falls.
  - Layer several low-alpha shadows. His example uses 5 layers at .075.
  - Colour-match the shadow instead of using black, which "desaturates" the ground. Use the background hue at lower saturation and lightness, and drive it with a `--shadow-color` variable.
  - He advises not animating layered shadows.
  - `filter: drop-shadow` follows alpha contours. His claim that it is "often faster" via hardware acceleration is **secondary**: no vendor source found.
- **Tobias Ahlin, *Smoother & sharper shadows with layered box-shadows* (19 Sep 2019).** https://tobiasahlin.com/blog/layered-smooth-box-shadows/. **Verified.**
  - A single box-shadow is "a blurred silhouette".
  - Doubling layers (1, 2, 4, 8, 16 px, offset = blur) approximate the way real shadows soften with distance.
  - Alpha per layer falls as the layer count rises: 4 layers at .15, 5 at .12, 6 at .11.
  - Decreasing alpha with distance gives a "sharp" shadow; increasing alpha gives a "diffuse" one.

### 5.2 Spec semantics

From the CSS Backgrounds and Borders Level 3 editor's draft, §6.1 (https://drafts.csswg.org/css-backgrounds/#box-shadow). **Verified.**

- **Blur.** The result must approximate "a Gaussian blur with a standard deviation equal to half the blur radius". So **σ = blur/2**, and the visible transition is "approximately twice the length of the blur radius… centered on the shadow's edge". A `16px` blur fades over about 32px, from 16px inside the edge to 16px outside.
- **Knockout.** "The shadow is drawn outside the border edge only: it is clipped inside the border-box."
  - A translucent surface (glass, or `backdrop-filter`) therefore shows *no* shadow through itself.
  - Its shadow is a rim only, and on a busy background that rim can read as a dark halo.
- **Order.** "The first shadow is on top and the others are layered behind."
- **Spread** grows or shrinks the shape before blur. Material's ambient layer uses positive spread so the halo grows without the shadow moving down.
- Box-shadow animatability is listed as an at-risk feature in the draft's status section.

### 5.3 Performance

- **Compositor-only properties.** Paul Lewis, web.dev (updated 2015-03-20). https://web.dev/articles/stick-to-compositor-only-properties-and-manage-layer-count. **Verified.** Only `transform` and `opacity` skip layout and paint. Layers cost memory: "Do not promote elements unnecessarily."
- **Blur is expensive to paint.** Basques & Andrew, web.dev (2020). https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide. **Verified.** "Anything that involves a blur (like a shadow, for example) takes longer to paint than drawing a red box." Restrict animation to opacity and transform.
- **Property combinations cost more than their parts.** Colt McAnlis, web.dev (2013). https://web.dev/articles/css-paint-times. **Verified.** Combinations "can have a greater paint time than the sum of their parts". box-shadow combined with border-radius was the example ("2.2C"). The numbers come from a 2013 Chrome build, so they are dated.
- **Animate opacity, not the shadow.** Tobias Ahlin (2015). https://tobiasahlin.com/blog/how-to-animate-box-shadow/. **Verified.** Put the raised shadow on a `::after` at opacity 0 and fade it in, instead of animating `box-shadow`, because "Animating a change of box-shadow will hurt performance." He reports no measured numbers.
  - Material Web *does* transition `box-shadow` and `opacity` on its pseudo-elements (`_elevation.scss`), so a vendor-adjacent library accepts the cost.
- **`filter: drop-shadow`.**
  - No current vendor engineering post found.
  - A 2012 public-fx thread (Chris Marrin, Apple) calls drop-shadow inside hardware filter chains hard and slow. https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-fx/2012JanMar/0027.html. **Secondary** (search digest, and dated).
  - Treat the claim that "drop-shadow is GPU-fast" as **unverified**.

## 6. Synthesis

### 6.1 Mechanisms worth a principle page

**M1. A shadow says how high, and the offset says it.**
- Perceived gap is linear in y-offset, about 1.3–1.4 × offset (Cavanagh 2021).
- Blur changes the magnitude by only about 8%, and direction by about 2%.
- Blur and darkness decide *whether* the patch is accepted as a shadow (Kersten 1997; Cavanagh's light-patch control; Ni 2004 on darkness).
- Counterintuitive consequence: a "heavy" shadow is usually a *long* one, with a big offset. It says the card hovers far off the desk, and making it lighter does not lower it.
- Systems encode the same thing: Fluent's key offset = blur/2; M3 changes shadow only when elevation changes (M2: "When a surface changes shape or scale, but its elevation remains the same, its shadow should not change").

**M2. Two shadows, two jobs: one draws the edge, one says the distance.**
- M2 says "Don't" to key-only or ambient-only shadows. Fluent combines key and ambient. In M3 web, the key layer stays a tight 1–4px at α.3 while the ambient layer grows.
- Windows replaces the key with a stroke. Material's low-vision research found outline and shadow equally help findability.
- So the edge job can be done by a hairline, which is what Post already does.
- The ambient layer is the only part that has to scale with height.
- The failure of a single shadow: one value can't be both crisp at the edge and soft at distance, which is Ahlin's "blurred silhouette".

**M3. In dark mode, depth is lightness.**
- A shadow can only remove light, and there is almost none to remove (Material Dark.md).
- The systems agree on raising the surface tone instead: M2 +5% at 1dp up to +16% at 24dp, logarithmic; M3 tones 4 → 22; Apple base versus elevated; Atlassian.
- Fluent is the outlier: it doubles the shadow alpha.
- A light halo is not a substitute: "Don't use light glows" (M2); in Cavanagh 2021 a light patch gives no depth.

**M4. Shadow strength should depend on what's behind, not on rank.**
- Apple's Liquid Glass shadow gets stronger over text and weaker over plain light backgrounds.
- Fluent recomputes alpha from the surface's luminance.
- M3 uses shadows only where a "patterned or visually busy" background defeats the hairline.
- This contradicts the token-ramp intuition that a higher level means a darker shadow. Fluent's low ramp keeps alpha flat across levels, and Comeau *lowers* opacity as height grows.
- The job is separation contrast against the local background. Height is carried by offset (M1).

**M5. One light, few levels.**
- Ramachandran: shape-from-shading assumes one light source.
- Kersten: the visual system assumes a stationary light, and reads shadow change as object movement.
- M3: "The fewer levels in your UI, the more power they have."
- In practice, every shadow on a page shares one direction and one offset-to-blur ratio (Comeau). Any change in a shadow reads as the object moving.
- Hover-raise should be +1 level and identical for all similar elements (M3).

### 6.2 Rules a reader can apply

| Rule | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Key y-offset vs blur | offset = blur / 2 | Fluent `shadows.ts` |
| Ambient layer | 0 offset, about 2px blur (8px at high levels), drawing the edge | Fluent `shadows.ts` |
| Or replace the key/edge shadow with a 1px hairline | — | Windows (Fluent page); M3 "hairline style" |

### 6.3 Before/After demo ideas

1. **The raise slider: offset versus blur.**
   - One card on a warm desk with two sliders, *offset* and *blur*. A readout shows "perceived lift ≈ 1.35 × offset".
   - Before: the blur slider alone barely moves the card, and the shadow just gets mushier.
   - After: the offset slider clearly lifts it.
   - Optional third toggle: flip the shadow to a light patch, and the lift disappears (Cavanagh Fig. 1).
   - Mechanism: M1.
2. **One shadow versus key + ambient (or hairline + ambient).**
   - The same raise from level 1 to level 4, rendered two ways.
   - Before: one `0 Ypx 2Ypx α` shadow, which either looks dirty at the edge or vague at height.
   - After: a crisp edge (a hairline, or a 2px ambient) plus a growing key at offset = blur/2.
   - Show the two at equal total darkness. Let the reader toggle each layer off to see the job each one does.
   - Mechanism: M2.
3. **Same card, light desk and dark desk.**
   - Before: the light-mode shadow carried into dark mode at the same alpha, so the card and the toolbar visually fuse with the ground.
   - After A: Fluent's approach, with alpha doubled.
   - After B: Material's approach, with the surface lightened (+5% at 1dp, +11% at 6dp) and a hairline.
   - Include a "glow" variant that is marked wrong.
   - Mechanism: M3.
4. **The floating pill over text.**
   - Post's tool pill hovers over the mail card. Scroll text under it.
   - Before: a fixed, faint `0 2px 6px α.05` pill shadow. Over text, the pill edge is lost. Because its offset (2px) is half the card's (4px), it also reads as sitting *closer* than the card sits to the desk.
   - After: the shadow strengthens while text is under the pill and relaxes over blank paper (Apple's adaptive shadow), with a larger offset if the pill is meant to sit clearly above.
   - Also the case for M4 and M1 together.

### 6.4 Folklore versus evidence

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| "Light comes from the top-left, so shadows go down-right." | **Partly evidence, but weak for cast shadows.** The prior exists for *shading* at about 20–26° left (Sun & Perona; Mamassian & Goutcher). For cast-shadow depth, direction moved perceived depth by about 1.6% (Cavanagh 2021), and the prior is overridden by "barely perceptible" cues (Morgenstern 2011). What *is* evidenced: one consistent light (Ramachandran; Kersten). Straight-down shadows, which every design system uses, lose nothing measurable. |
| "Blurrier shadow means higher." | **Mostly folklore for magnitude.** It is physically true (penumbra grows with distance), but blur changed perceived depth by about 8% while offset dominated (Cavanagh). Blur helps the patch read *as* a shadow. |
| "Flat design hurts usability, so add shadows." | **Evidence for strong signifiers, not for shadows specifically.** NN/g 2017 (+22% time) and Burmistrov 2015 bundle shadows with gradients and other cues. Material's research treats outline and shadow as equivalent. |
| "A higher level needs a darker shadow." | **Folklore.** The systems keep alpha flat (Fluent's low ramp) or lower it with height (Comeau). Height comes from offset. |
| "Shadows should be black." | **Folklore.** Black desaturates the ground (Comeau). Fluent adjusts alpha by surface luminance. Apple lets colour bleed into the shadow ("light… bleeds into the shadow"). |
| "In dark mode, use a glow." | **Contradicted.** M2 says "Don't use light glows". A light patch gives no depth (Cavanagh; Kersten demos). |
| "Shadow = importance." | **Contested.** Fluent says it conveys importance. M3 says it is distance, and its power comes from scarcity. No study measures shadow and perceived importance directly. |
| "box-shadow is slow; drop-shadow is GPU-fast." | **Half-evidence.** Blur paint cost and the advice to avoid animating `box-shadow` are vendor-documented (web.dev). The claim that `drop-shadow` is fast has no vendor source found. |
| "Layered shadows look more realistic." | **Craft consensus, not measured.** Ahlin and Comeau argue it from physics (penumbra sums). No perception study compares layered with single shadows in UI. |
