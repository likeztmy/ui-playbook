# Component: Icon

Status: signed off 2026-10-29 ("As proposed"): the site moves to Lucide's own stroke; four demos, emoji as text.

Research: `docs/research/2026-10-29-icons.md` (sections cited as §n). Second topic of
`docs/plans/2026-10-29-direction-systems.md`.

## Summary line (draft)

From the one library the product uses, sized and weighted like the text beside it. Never a symbol
or an emoji, never drawn for the occasion.

## Sections

**Live demo at the top.** A toolbar and a sidebar built with Lucide, each icon-only button naming
itself in a tooltip.

**When to use.** An icon sits beside a label to make a row or a menu item quicker to find. It
stands alone only on the few controls convention has fixed (close, search, more, a toolbar's tools),
and then names itself in a tooltip and `aria-label`. Navigation keeps its words.

**Details**, each with a before/after demo:

1. *A symbol isn't an icon.* The same menu with ↗ ✓ ★ ✕ typed as text, against Lucide. The
   demo shows what each one is announced as (↗ "up-right arrow", ☰ "trigram for heaven"), and that
   the site's own fonts don't contain them, so the system draws them from another font (§4, §5).
2. *An emoji is the vendor's picture.* Even on the same phone, people disagree in a quarter of cases
   about whether an emoji is positive, neutral or negative (§3). As an icon it means something
   different to each reader. Text only, no demo.
3. *Most icons need their word.* A sidebar icon-only against icon and label. Icon-only starts much
   worse and catches up only with use (§1). Navigation hidden behind an icon was used 27% of the
   time on desktop against 48% when shown, with tasks at least 39% slower (§2, NN/g's own study).
   How realistic the drawing is matters least; how well it matches the function, and familiarity,
   matter most (§1). So the library's standard glyph beats a clever new one.
4. *Weight follows the text.* A 24px icon at its default stroke beside 14px text against a 16px one:
   the first is 1.7× heavier than the letters (§6). Apple and Material solve the same problem with
   weights matched to the text's.
5. *Drawn for the occasion.* The eval's hand-drawn eye, tick and arrow, against Lucide's. No study
   measures this (§7), so the page says what the systems say and shows it, without claiming a cost.

**Kinds.** Beside a label · alone, in an icon button · status (a filled or outline variant per
state) · decorative (`aria-hidden`).

**Anatomy table.** Size 16px (14 in dense rows; 20 in the Consumer app system), the library's own
stroke, `currentColor`, 8px from its label, the target 24/32/44 by system.

**States.** Rest in muted ink, hover and selected in full ink, selected may switch to the filled
variant (Apple, Material, Fluent §7), disabled at the disabled ink.

**Common mistakes.** Symbols and emoji · two libraries, or outline and filled mixed at rest ·
icon-only navigation · a decorative icon announced, or an icon button with no name · an icon
24px beside 14px text.

**Copy.** The tooltip and `aria-label` are the same words. A decorative icon says nothing.

## Decisions for the owner

1. The site's own icons are Lucide at 1.5px with a non-scaling stroke: 1.5px at every size, against
   about 1.2px for Geist's stems at 14px (§6). That's a little heavier than the text. Keep 1.5 as
   the house choice, and say so on the page? Or move the site to Lucide's own stroke, which comes
   out at 1.33px at 16px and matches Geist's regular weight? (Proposed: move to Lucide's own
   stroke, since the page will teach "as heavy as the text" and the site should meet it.)
2. Four demos are proposed; the emoji detail is text only. Enough, or fewer?
