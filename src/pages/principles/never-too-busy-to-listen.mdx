## The rule

**An animation is never too busy to listen.** The next click can come at any moment, and it means the person has changed their mind. The motion should turn from wherever it is, at once, towards the new target, keeping the speed it had. Whether it can depends on how it was built: a lock that drops clicks, or keyframes, can't turn at all; a transition turns from where it is but starts again from rest; only a spring keeps the speed it had.

## When it doesn't apply

- **A sequence that teaches.** A one-time walkthrough whose steps must be seen in order can finish its step before taking the next.
- **Reduced motion.** With the motion gone, there's nothing to turn; the state simply changes.

## Where the playbook applies it

- [Switch](/components/switch) and [Segmented control](/components/segmented-control): the thumb is a transition, so a quick second press sends it back from where it is.
- [Popover](/components/popover) and [Menu](/components/menu): closing mid-way reverses from the current frame.
- [Hover forgives](/principles/hover-forgives): the pointer's intent, judged while it's still moving.
