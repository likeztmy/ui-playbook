## The rule

**Nothing moves unless it was asked to.** People aim before they click and read before they scroll; a layout that moves under them sends the click somewhere else and loses their place. The browser's own measure of a bad shift says the same: it leaves out any shift within 500ms of a click, a tap or a key, because a change that answers the user is expected. Only the unasked move counts.

## Measure it

Chrome and Edge measure every shift as it happens: how much of the window moved, times how far ([Layout Instability API](https://web.dev/articles/cls)). Under each demo is that measure for the demo alone: how many shifts the browser saw, and their score, leaving out shifts within 500ms of input, as CLS does. A page should stay at 0.1 or less across its whole life; a small box moving a few pixels scores almost nothing, and it takes only one to put the wrong link under the pointer. Other browsers don't report shifts, so there the count is hidden.

The measure only sees boxes that move. A scroll bar that comes and goes after typing, or a shift that lands within half a second of a click, scores nothing and can still put the wrong row under the pointer.

## When it doesn't apply

- **A move the user asked for.** Opening a section, loading more, archiving a row: the layout should follow at once.
- **Motion that doesn't move the layout.** A transform scales or slides a thing without pushing anything else; it is animation, not a shift.

## Where the playbook applies it

- [Button](/components/button): a button keeps its width while it loads.
- [Tabs](/components/tabs): the current tab changes colour and bar, not weight.
- [Slider](/components/slider) and [Card](/components/card): digits keep their width as values change.
- [Where the eye goes](/principles/where-the-eye-goes): a count that changes moves where the eye can see it.
