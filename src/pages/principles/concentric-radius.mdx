## The rule

**Inner radius = outer radius − inset − border.** When both arcs share a centre, the gap at the corner is as wide as the gap along the sides. Copying the outer radius inward makes the inner shape look swollen at its corners; making it square makes it look pasted on.

<ConcentricTweak client:visible />

The formula holds while the inset is smaller than the outer radius. Once the inset reaches it, the two arcs no longer touch and the rule has nothing to keep equal: give the inner shape its own usual radius. A dialog with an 18px corner and 20px padding keeps its pill buttons, not square ones.

## Precision's pairings

The radius scale steps in 2–4px so the common insets always land on a token.

| Outer | Inset | Inner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Card `radius-xl` 18 | `space-2` 8 | Stage `radius-lg` 10 |
| Property panel `radius-xl` 18 | `space-2` 8 | Slider `radius-lg` 10 |
| Menu `radius-lg` 10 | `space-1` 4 | Item `radius-sm` 6 |
| Field `radius-lg` 10 | 3 + 1px border | Key `radius-sm` 6 |
| Toolbar, segmented, toast `radius-full` | 2 or 4 | `radius-full` |
| Switch track `radius-full` | 2 | Knob `radius-full` |
| Dialog `radius-xl` 18 | `space-5` 20 | Buttons, their own pill |

A pill in a pill is concentric by construction, as long as the inset is equal on every side, which is why Precision makes toolbars, segmented controls and toasts pills. The dialog is the exception above: its padding exceeds its radius, so its buttons keep their own shape.

## Common mistakes

- **Uneven inset.** Concentric corners need the same inset on both edges that meet at the corner. A toolbar padded 4 × 12 can't be concentric.
- **One radius for everything.** A single global radius guarantees every nested pair is wrong.
