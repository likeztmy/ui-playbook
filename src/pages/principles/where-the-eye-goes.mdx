## The rule

**The eye guesses before it reads.** It watches the spot it's working on and misses what changes elsewhere. It stops where the page looks finished, skips whatever looks like an ad, sorts by distance before it reads a word, and follows the label that promises what it came for. A layout that guides works with those guesses.

## Check it yourself

<EyeSquint client:visible />

Blur a screen until the words go, and what's left is what the eye lands on first: the [squint test](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/squint-test/). It measures weight, not trust. Blurred, the promo band is the loudest thing on the Billing page, and it's still the part people skip.

## When it doesn't apply

- **Long reading.** An article read from the top is read, not scanned; calm text with headings serves it better than a ladder of weights.
- **A change the user is waiting for.** When the eye is already on the cart, say after opening it, a count that changes in place is seen without help.
