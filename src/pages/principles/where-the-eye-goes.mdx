## The rule

**Give the eye one way in, then steps down.** People don't read a screen, they scan it: they land on the strongest thing, sort what's near into groups, skim the first words of each line, and follow whichever label promises what they came for. A layout that guides does it with weight, space and words, before any arrow or hint.

## When it doesn't apply

- **Long reading.** An article wants calm text with headings, not a ladder of weights; scanning patterns are for pages people work in.
- **Loud isn't strong.** A heading or notice styled like a promotion, bright and boxed, gets skipped as one ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/banner-blindness-old-and-new-findings/)). The way in is the strongest thing in the interface's own voice.

## Where the playbook applies it

- [Input](/components/input): a label above, one line under, nothing that competes with the field.
- [Menu](/components/menu): groups by separators, the destructive one last.
- [Switch](/components/switch): labels on one edge, switches in one column.
- [Table](/components/table): secondary columns step back, so names lead.
