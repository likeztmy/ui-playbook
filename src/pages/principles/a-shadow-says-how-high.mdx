## What a shadow is for

**A shadow says that one surface lies over another, and could come away from it.** That is all it says. Cast shadows are how vision places an object against what's behind it ([Mamassian, Knill & Kersten, 1998](https://doi.org/10.1016/s1364-6613(98)01204-2)), and Material names the same three things a surface has to show: its edges, its overlap with other surfaces, and its distance from them ([Material 3, Elevation](https://m3.material.io/styles/elevation/overview)).

Take that cue away and things meant to be acted on stop looking like it. In NN/g's eyetracking, pages whose controls had lost their depth, shadows among other cues, took people 22% longer, with 25% more fixations ([NN/g, 2017](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/flat-ui-less-attention-cause-uncertainty/)). Material's interviews with low-vision users found that shadows and outlines make a component faster to find when scanning ([Material 2, Light and shadows](https://m2.material.io/design/environment/light-shadows.html)).

## When to use one

Where a surface really is over something:

- **It covers what's under it:** a menu, a popover, a dialog, a toast, a toolbar floating over a canvas. The shadow says the page goes on beneath, and is there again when this goes.
- **It has been picked up:** a dragged card, a row being reordered. Material keeps its highest two levels for hover and drag, things in the hand ([Material 3, Elevation](https://m3.material.io/styles/elevation/overview)).
- **It is one thing to act on, lying on a ground:** a card that opens, the mail lying on its desk in [Post](/practice/mail).

And not:

- **To group.** Things that belong together share space or a fill ([Space groups by ratio](/principles/space-groups-by-ratio)); a shadow on each would say each could be lifted off alone. Rows, cells and fields sit in the page, not on it.
- **To rank.** A higher surface is only nearer; a shadow that grows to make something look important says it's floating away. Material treats elevation as distance, and warns against changing a component’s usual elevation.
- **To part a surface from a plain ground.** A hairline or a step in tone does that. Material 3 keeps the shadow "only when required to create additional protection against a background or to encourage interaction" ([Material 3, Applying elevation](https://m3.material.io/styles/elevation/applying-elevation)): a busy or patterned ground, or a lift on interaction.

## How high: the offset

**A shadow says how high, and its offset is what says it.** Cavanagh, Casati and Elder had 25 people judge how far a square floated above its ground. Every person's answers rose in a straight line with the shadow's offset, at about 1.3 times it; blur and the light's direction left them "relatively unaffected" ([Cavanagh, Casati & Elder, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1167/jov.21.12.15)). A light patch in the shadow's place lifted nothing. So a shadow that looks heavy is usually a long one, and lightening it doesn't lower the card: shorten the drop.

Blur and darkness decide something else: whether the patch is taken for a shadow at all. A moving shadow alone makes a ball seem to rise off the floor ([Kersten, Mamassian & Knill, 1997](https://doi.org/10.1068/p260171)); turn the patch light and the effect weakens ([Kersten lab, demos](https://kerstenlab.psych.umn.edu/demos/shadows)).

## Details

### Two shadows, two jobs

<ShadowLayers client:visible />

Material draws a key shadow for the edge and an ambient one for the distance, and marks either alone as a don't ([Material 2, Light and shadows](https://m2.material.io/design/environment/light-shadows.html)).

### In the dark, depth is lightness

<ShadowDesks client:visible />

A shadow can only take light away, and a near-black desk has none to spare: the light-mode shadow vanishes, and a darker one only adds a muddy halo. Material, Apple and Atlassian raise a surface by lightening it. Material's overlay goes from 5% white one step up to 16% at the top, and it says plainly, "Don't use light glows in place of dark shadows" ([Material 2, Dark theme](https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme.html); [Apple, Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode); [Atlassian, Elevation](https://atlassian.design/foundations/elevation)).

### One light, few heights

Every shadow on screen comes from one light: one direction, and one colour, the desk's own hue rather than black, which greys whatever it falls on ([Josh Comeau, Designing beautiful shadows](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/css/designing-shadows/)). And few heights: "The fewer levels in your UI, the more power they have to direct attention" ([Material 3, Elevation](https://m3.material.io/styles/elevation/applying-elevation)). A hover lifts a thing one step, never two.

## The playbook's heights

Each step doubles the drop; the darkness stays between 6% and 8%. The blur is twice the offset, as in Fluent's ramp ([Fluent UI, shadows](https://github.com/microsoft/fluentui/blob/master/packages/tokens/src/utils/shadows.ts)). A hairline draws every edge, as Windows does with a stroke.

| Height | Used by | Drop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `shadow-xs` | A card at rest, the segmented control's thumb | `0 1px 2px` |
| `shadow-sm` | A toolbar, a card under the pointer | `0 2px 4px` |
| `shadow-md` | A menu, a popover | `0 6px 12px` |
| `shadow-lg` | A dialog, a toast | `0 12px 24px` |

In dark mode the same steps keep their hairline, and the raised surface is the lighter one.

## When it doesn't apply

- **Glass.** A box's shadow is clipped under the box itself, so a translucent surface shows only a rim of it ([CSS Backgrounds](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-backgrounds/#box-shadow)); its edge has to come from a stroke.

## Where the playbook applies it

- [Card](/components/card): resting at `shadow-xs`; under the pointer it lifts one step, to `shadow-sm`.
- [Menu](/components/menu) and [Popover](/components/popover): `shadow-md`, a step above the toolbar or field that opened them.
- [Dialog](/components/dialog) and [Toast](/components/toast): `shadow-lg`.
- [Mail](/practice/mail): the mail is raised one step off its desk, and its floating tools a step above that, with the longer drop and a hairline that keeps their edge over the text scrolling beneath.
