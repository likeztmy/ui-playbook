## The rule

**A gap only separates when it's clearly bigger than the gaps around it.** What belongs together is settled by comparing distances, not by any one of them: a header, a label or a heading joins whichever neighbour is nearer. Give it more space on the side it doesn't belong to. And a shared background or border outranks spacing altogether.

## Details

### More space doesn't fix it

<RatioScale client:visible />

Doubling every gap leaves the header just as torn: above ÷ below stays at 1.0×, so the confusion stays. Grouping by nearness depends "only on the relative distance" between elements, and holds when the whole pattern is scaled ([Wagemans et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/), reviewing Kubovy's lattices). When a layout reads wrong, move space from one side to the other before adding any.

## When it doesn't apply

- **Real columns.** In a table, a wide gap between columns is meant to make columns; the rows hold together through their alignment and their lines, not their spacing.
- **A single group.** A short list with no headers has one gap to get right, the one between its items.

## Where the playbook applies it

- [Input](/components/input): label, field and hint sit 4 apart, so a form that spaces its fields 16 or more keeps each label with its own box.
