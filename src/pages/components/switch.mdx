## When to use

A switch turns a setting on or off, and the change happens the moment it flips: no Save, no confirmation. Use it for:

- **Settings that apply at once.** *Autosave*, *Show grid*, *Notifications*.
- **A setting that governs others.** A switch has more weight than a checkbox, so Apple suggests it for the main setting over a group.

Reach for something else when:

- **The choice waits for Save or Submit.** That's a Checkbox.
- **The change is risky.** A switch can't ask first. Use a Button, and confirm if it can't be undone.
- **There are more than two states.** A segmented control or a Select.
- **It's in a toolbar.** Apple keeps switches in the window body; a toolbar uses a toggle button.

### Switch or Checkbox

| | Switch | Checkbox |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Takes effect | Immediately | When the form is sent |
| Typical home | Settings lists | Forms, lists of options |
| Mixed state | None | A dash, for a parent |
| Hierarchy | Master over a group | Parent with indented children |
| Placement | Trailing in lists, leading in forms | Leading, always |

## Common mistakes

- **A switch in a form with a Submit button.** The form makes people wonder whether flipping it did anything yet.
- **On / Off text inside or beside the track.** The knob's side and the fill already say it; text doubles the reading and breaks in other languages.
- **A confirmation after flipping.** If it needs one, it shouldn't be a switch.
- **Colour as the only difference.** The knob's position carries the state too, so it survives colour blindness and greyscale.

## Copy

- Label: the setting, as a noun phrase or a verb phrase that's true when on: **Autosave**, **Show grid**.
- Never the state: not *Grid on*, not *Enabled*.
- Description: what on means, when it isn't obvious: **Moved objects land on grid lines.**
