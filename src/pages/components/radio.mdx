## When to use

A radio group picks exactly one of a few options, all in sight, and the choice waits for the form to be sent. Use it for:

- **Two to five options worth reading side by side.** *Standard*, *Express*, *Overnight*, each with its price.
- **Options that need a line of explanation.** A description under each label says what it does.
- **A choice that needs one more value.** *Custom width* shows its field only while chosen.

Reach for something else when:

- **There are more than five.** A Select keeps the list out of the way until it's needed.
- **It switches a view at once.** A Segmented control, in a toolbar or a header.
- **Any number can be on.** Checkboxes.
- **It's one option, on or off.** A Checkbox in a form, a Switch for a setting.

## Common mistakes

- **No default.** Nothing chosen is a state the user can't get back to. Choose the safe option, or add *None* as a radio.
- **A radio that acts on the spot.** If choosing changes the view at once, it's a Segmented control.
- **Yes and No as radios.** A single Checkbox says the same in one row: **Email me a receipt**.
- **Hiding the choice in a Select.** Three options with prices read better all in sight than one at a time.

## Copy

- Group label: what is being chosen: **Shipping**, **Export at**.
- Option labels: parallel, short, sentence case, no full stop: **Your team**, **Anyone with the link**.
- Description: what the choice means, not a restatement: **Including guests, once they sign in.**
