## When to use

A segmented control picks one of two to five options that change a view or a mode, with every option in sight: *Board*, *List*, *Timeline*. Reach for something else when:

- **It switches regions of a page.** Overview, Activity, Settings are Tabs.
- **There are more than five options, or long ones.** A Select.
- **It's on or off.** A Switch: *Show grid* isn't a choice between *On* and *Off*.
- **The options are actions.** Buttons in a Toolbar. Apple: keep a control all choices or all actions, never a mix.

### Which control

| | Segmented control | Tabs | Select | Switch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chooses | A mode or a view | A region of the page | A value | On or off |
| Options | 2–5, all visible | 2–6, can scroll | 5–15, in a list | One |
| Sits | Beside what it changes | Above what it changes | In a form or panel | In a list or form |

## Common mistakes

- **A label that repeats the options.** *View: Board / List* needs no *View*; the words say it. Name the group for assistive tech with `aria-label`.
- **Six segments on a narrow screen.** Past five, labels truncate and targets shrink. Use a Select.
- **Segments that do something.** If a press doesn't leave a segment chosen, it's a button row.
- **Changing the page's main content.** That's what Tabs are for; a segmented control belongs in a toolbar or an inspector.

## Copy

- Segments: nouns, one word where possible, of similar length: **Board**, **List**, **Timeline**.
- Sentence case. Icon segments take the same nouns as tooltips: **Align left**.
